# 4K 120 and 10-bit (Phase 3) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Stream 3840x2160 at 120 fps in 10-bit HEVC whenever the TV's display and decoder can take it, and fall back on their own to 60 Hz, 8 bits or AVC when they cannot.

**Architecture:** The TV picks the largest picture at 120 Hz (else 60 Hz) that a hardware decoder sustains, and tells the server in the Hello which codecs it decodes in 10 bits. The server shows the Parsec virtual display at the lowest refresh that is a multiple of the request (240 Hz for 120, since the driver has no 120 Hz mode at 4K) and captures at the requested rate. It encodes 10-bit HEVC with NVENC P1 above the 4K60 pixel rate and scales the bitrate by √(fps/60). The frame clock waits a quarter interval past each turn before repeating, so a picture that arrives a little late is still encoded on arrival.

**Tech Stack:** Rust 2024 workspace (`app`, `nvenc`, `desktop`, `wire`, `media`), cargo-nextest, clippy, NVENC C layer; Kotlin Android TV app (JUnit 4, Gradle).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-26-native-pipeline-design.md` (Phase 3, line 201; TV display mode, line 187).

- HEVC level 5.2 Main tier: luma rate ≤ 1,069,547,520 samples/s, bitrate ≤ 60,000 kbps. 4K120 is 995,328,000 samples/s.
- 4K120 frame budget is 8.33 ms. NVENC on the RTX 5070 encodes 4K HEVC in about 3.8 ms at P1 and 7.4–7.6 ms at P4, so P1 above 4K60.
- The Parsec VDD offers 24, 30, 60, 144 and 240 Hz at 3840x2160. There is no 120 Hz mode.
- Zero manual input: every new setting has a default that works; the TV decides everything from its own capabilities.
- The TV self-update path (`ConnectActivity` → `UpdateRunner`) is not touched.

## Review Focus

1. A TV whose decoder lists a Main10 profile but fails on 10-bit streams: it must fall back to 8-bit HEVC before it gives up HEVC for AVC (Task 2, `ADecoderThatKeepsFailingOnTenBitHevcFallsBackToEightBitsFirstTest`).
2. A TV with 50, 100 or 144 Hz modes: the server cannot show those evenly, so the TV must stay on its 120 or 60 Hz modes (Task 1, `rates the server cannot match evenly are skipped`).
3. A desktop picture arriving just after its turn, through scheduler jitter at 60/60 or 2:1 decimation from 240 Hz: it must be encoded on arrival, not replaced by a repeat of the older picture (Task 6, `a_picture_arriving_just_after_its_turn_is_waited_for_test.rs`).
4. A request the virtual display cannot match exactly: 120 Hz must be shown at 240 Hz and captured at 120 fps, not shown at 60 Hz (Task 7).
5. Extreme but allowed settings: an 8K 240 Hz mode must not overflow the pixel-rate arithmetic, and `VIDEO_BITRATE_KBPS=200000` at 240 fps must stay within NVENC's 800,000 kbps limit (Task 5).
